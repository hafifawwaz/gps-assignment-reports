# Discussion citation checks

<p class="doc-meta"><span class="chip chip-note">Note</span> <span class="chip chip-project">Master</span> <span class="chip chip-date">23-07-2026</span></p>

Every reference in the discussion notes was checked against the text versions of the papers in the literature library (not the PDFs). The question each time: does the paper really say what the note claims?

- [Positioning against deterministic assignment](m-positioning-vs-deterministic-assignment.md): confirmed for Alvares et al. (the "inside the outline" rule) and Wu et al. (the nearest-place rule, and the chance of a wrong answer depending on GPS error, map layout and distance to the edge). No paper is cited for "flexible models fit better on training data but worse on new data". That statement stays the thesis's own finding from research question 3.
- [Extending map matching to stationary stays](m-map-matching-extension.md): all four papers were read in full. None of them handles a person standing still.
- [Tension with the street-space argument](m-street-space-tension.md): the search found **nothing**. Six possible sources were checked (Iwata et al., Merry and Bettinger, Brakatsoulas et al., Yan et al., Okabe, and Jacobs, Gehl and Whyte on how streets are represented). None supports "long place types such as streets are harder for methods that count nearby candidates". The thesis says openly that this is a gap.
- [Candidate density as boundary-matching difficulty](m-boundary-matching-analogy.md): the numbers from Weng et al. and three passages from Quddus et al. were confirmed. Kim and Park, Merry and Bettinger, and Brakatsoulas et al. were considered but not used.

Problems found in the reference list but not yet fixed: Brakatsoulas et al. appears twice. Yan et al. (2010) is saved under the key `hutchison_hybrid_2010`, also twice. An earlier check said there was no text file for Okabe (2000), but the file exists.
