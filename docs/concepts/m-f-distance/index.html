    f_distance

    Concept Master 25-07-2026

    How close the candidate is to the center of the stay. The score is 1 when the candidate is at the center and falls toward 0 as it gets farther away. How fast it falls depends on how spread out the stay's points are (\(\sigma_i\)) and on a setting \(\alpha\):

    \[f_{\text{distance}}(E_k, C_i) = \exp\left(-\frac{d_{ki}^2}{2(\alpha \cdot \sigma_i)^2}\right)\]

    \(d_{ki}\) is the distance from the stay's medoid to candidate \(E_k\), and 0 when the medoid lies inside it. The medoid is the GPS point closest to the average position of the stay. \(\sigma_i\) is the spread of the stay's points (sigma), with a minimum of 5 m. When the points are widely spread, the GPS position is less certain, so the score falls more slowly and farther places still get some credit.

    In the models: \(\alpha\) was 1.0 (not tuned) until Alpha calibration set it to 2.764. Before that, its weight changed sign between the 10 m and 15 m search distances (Model 1: base, Model 2: share-type and sigma). A squared version of this score also looked useful, but only because α was wrong (The f_distance² artifact).
