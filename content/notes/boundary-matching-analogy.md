# Candidate density as boundary-matching difficulty

<p class="doc-meta"><span class="chip chip-note">Note</span> <span class="chip chip-date">23-07-2026</span></p>

A narrower analogue exists in the positional-error literature. Weng et al. (2025) need sidewalk matching because sidewalks are about 2 m wide while raw urban-canyon error reaches 18.4 m RMS static and up to 32 m cross-street dynamic. Quddus et al. (2007) note that point-to-curve matching is unstable at high road density, belief-theory matching degrades on parallel streets, and Y-junctions are ambiguous when both links are almost equally close.

[Candidate density bias toward building](../analysis/candidate-density-bias.md) is a similar problem one level up: densely packed same-type candidates give small noise more chances to land on the wrong one.

The limit of the analogy: those papers resolve between alternatives of the same kind, while the street disadvantage here is a **between-type** competition (street losing to building). The shared root is closely packed candidates, not an identical mechanism.
